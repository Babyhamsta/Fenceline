What Is a Proxy Server? A Plain-English Explainer

A proxy server is an intermediary computer that sits between a user's device and the wider internet. When you request a web page, the request goes to the proxy first, which forwards it on your behalf and returns the response. Organisations use proxies for caching, access control, and monitoring.

There are several kinds of proxy. A forward proxy serves internal clients reaching out to the internet. A reverse proxy sits in front of servers to balance load and add caching. A transparent proxy intercepts traffic without client configuration, which is how many school and workplace content filters are implemented.

Proxies are frequently confused with VPNs. Both relay traffic, but a VPN encrypts the entire connection at the operating-system level, whereas a typical web proxy only handles a single application's traffic and may not encrypt it at all.

Understanding how a proxy works helps administrators design networks and helps students understand why certain sites are blocked at school. This article is purely educational and does not provide tools to circumvent any filter.
